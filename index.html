Fruita & Moab 2026

Five riding days in two desert riding towns, and the plan changed along the way. David picked up Adam in Grand Junction on the night of the 23rd, and rain reshuffled the Fruita days: Kokopelli on Saturday, then 18 Road on Sunday. Adam flew home Monday, so David drove to Moab and rode Klondike Bluffs solo. John joined for the last two days: the shuttle-assisted descent David now counts among his top 3 downhill runs of all time, Slickrock, a sunset hike to Delicate Arch, and a final ride at Bar M. Then John drove east and David drove west, home.

The Moab Cyclery shuttle drove us about 3,000 ft up, roughly three-quarters of the way. Plans · 2027

Next year: back with our wives

We plan to come back next year with our wives, with more of the trip spent hiking in Arches National Park. The sunset hike to Delicate Arch made the case: the park's scale and beauty are worth a visit on their own.

Arches works well for this because each trailhead is the starting point for a comfortable day hike to an individual arch or an odd rock formation. Drive the park road, park, hike out and back, then move on to the next one. Several short hikes fit in a day, with time left over for a ride or a rest.

Hike

Short hikes from the trailheads

  • Delicate Arch: about 3 mi round trip, best at sunset. We did it this year
  • The Windows and Double Arch: short, easy walks to several big arches
  • Park Avenue: about 1 mi one way between tall fins of rock
  • Balanced Rock: a short loop around the formation, right off the road
  • Landscape Arch: about 2 mi round trip from the Devils Garden trailhead
Longer

For a bigger day

  • Devils Garden: keep going past Landscape Arch to more arches; the full loop is around 8 mi
  • Fiery Furnace: a maze of narrow sandstone fins; needs a permit or a ranger-led tour
Check

Before we book

  • Arches has required timed-entry reservations for much of the year; check the NPS Plan Your Visit page for 2027
  • Distances above are approximate; confirm them on the NPS trail pages
  • Start early or go late: trailhead parking fills up, and there's little shade
  • Late October worked well: cool mornings, warm afternoons, snow on the La Sals
How we ride

Ride style

Both of us are advanced riders. Anything in Truckee is fair game, but we aren't Northstar jumpers or speed demons. The plan goes after technical, flowy, scenic riding and stays away from exposure and high-consequence features.

In

What we're after

  • Technical climbs and rock moves
  • Long, flowy singletrack
  • Big views from the trail, not from a cliff edge
Out

What we're skipping

  • Rim riding with fall exposure
  • Double-black drop lines
  • Big jumps and race-pace descents
  • Anything we'd walk anyway: walk it
Bikes

E-bikes

We rode Class 1 Specialized Levos: David on a Levo SL with two range extenders, about 4,000 ft of climbing at 70% assist, and Adam on a borrowed Levo Gen 3.

Good news: Class 1 e-bikes are now allowed in almost all the riding areas around Fruita and Moab.

But check every route before you commit to it. E-bike rules change from year to year, and a few trails stay closed. The fines are high: this year, the top singletrack above the Moab Cyclery shuttle drop carried a $5,000 fine plus bike confiscation for riding an e-bike. Start with the BLM Moab Field Office e-bike page, look for the e-bike stickers on trail signs, and ask the local shops.
