Fruita & Moab 2026

Five riding days in two desert riding towns, and the plan changed along the way. David picked up Adam in Grand Junction on the night of the 23rd, and rain reshuffled the Fruita days: Kokopelli on Saturday, then 18 Road on Sunday. Adam flew home Monday, so David drove to Moab and rode Klondike Bluffs solo. John joined for the last two days: the shuttle-assisted descent David now counts among his top 3 downhill runs of all time, Slickrock, a sunset hike to Delicate Arch, and a final ride at Bar M. Then John drove east and David drove west, home.

How we ride

Ride style

Both of us are advanced riders. Anything in Truckee is fair game, but we aren't Northstar jumpers or speed demons. The plan goes after technical, flowy, scenic riding and stays away from exposure and high-consequence features.

In

What we're after

  • Technical climbs and rock moves
  • Long, flowy singletrack
  • Big views from the trail, not from a cliff edge
Out

What we're skipping

  • Rim riding with fall exposure
  • Double-black drop lines
  • Big jumps and race-pace descents
  • Anything we'd walk anyway: walk it
Bikes

Advice from Claude, the planning assistant

E-bikes on closed trails

Several Fruita shops have said it's fine to ride a low-powered Class 1 e-bike on trails that are closed to e-bikes, as long as we're respectful. The reasoning makes sense: a Levo SL at 70% assist rides much like a fit rider on an acoustic bike, and fit acoustic riders are often faster. That gap is a big part of why Class 1 is the class land managers are opening trails to.

Here's the concern.

Rule

Shops can't give permission

  • On BLM land, a closure to e-bikes is a federal rule, and rangers can write tickets.
  • "A shop said it was fine" won't help if we're stopped.
  • Moab's trail signs now carry stickers marking which trails allow e-bikes, so the closures are visible and easy to enforce.
Access

The closures were chosen deliberately

  • Moab BLM opened about 200 miles to Class 1 e-bikes in March 2026 and deliberately left out a few trails, including Eagle Eye, Porcupine Rim Singletrack and LPS.
  • Grand Junction kept Lunch Loops closed in the same round.
  • More access depends on showing that the current rules hold up.
Cost

The legal plan barely loses anything

  • Raptor Route: start at Hawks Glide instead of Eagle Eye.
  • Lunch Loops: 18 Road covers the same kind of riding, and it allows e-bikes.
  • Kokopelli: officially closed to e-bikes, so the third riding day moves to Moab, which has more open trails. Over The Edge says riders use e-bikes there anyway (a gray area); that is our call to make.

My advice: ride the plan as written. It only uses trails open to Class 1 e-bikes, and it still covers the best riding in both towns. For any trail that's still closed, the decision is yours to make on the day, knowing the risk. This page is a shared plan, so it sticks to what's allowed.

Adam flew home today, so David drove to Moab and rode solo. “Very technical riding here, my arms and shoulders said hello. Missing Adam today big time!” Rocky red-dirt singletrack and slickrock, with views out over the valley. See it on Strava ↗

Planned: Navajo Rocks Loop, about 17.1 mi and 1,600 ft.

1,654 ft of climbing, 41% of range

Tue 27

Ridden: “Moab: Top 3 DH” · with John

Actual: 34.60 mi · 4,351 ft of climbing · 3 h 8 min · 1 Strava achievement

The Moab Cyclery shuttle drove us about 3,000 ft up, roughly three-quarters of the way. We climbed the rest to the top, then turned around and descended the way we came to skip the top singletrack, which is closed to e-bikes and carries a $5,000 fine and bike confiscation. Then the long descent back to town: one of David's top 3 downhill runs of all time. Technical, with warning signs to dismount on the crazy stuff; firm dirt; epic views in every direction, with the snowy La Sals behind the slickrock. “The scale of this place is like no other, freaking amazing!” Strava: 9th on the “Stop sign to FS boundary” segment. See it on Strava ↗

Near the bottom we added the Slickrock trails: very strange riding, steep, and the sandstone gripped in a way David didn't think was possible.

Because the shuttle did most of the climbing, we still had time and legs to hike in Arches National Park later that day.

Afternoon sunset hike: Delicate Arch · with John · 2.92 mi · 686 ft of climbing · 1 h 2 min. Delicate Arch in late light, with the snowy La Sals framed through it. “Ok, hikes like this make me ❤️ hiking. Need to rethink always biking, thanks JC!” See it on Strava ↗

Planned: partial Raptor Route from Hawks Glide + Slickrock Practice Loop, about 800 ft of climbing.

Strava's 4,351 ft includes about 3,000 ft on the shuttle, so roughly 1,350 ft was pedaled: about 34% of range

Wed 28

Ridden: “Moab: party over 😕”, Bar M trails · with John

Actual: 13.73 mi · 1,136 ft of climbing · 1 h 45 min · started 10:28 am

The last ride of the trip, at Moab Brands. A surprisingly physical challenge, with a long, techy slickrock section: some chunky stretches and some nice flowy singletrack. Afterwards John drove east and David drove west, home to Truckee. “Putting away bike soon with snow expected next week?” See it on Strava ↗
